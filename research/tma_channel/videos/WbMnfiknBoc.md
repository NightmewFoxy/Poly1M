## Mechanical rules (only what the video actually states)
Format: 16 hand-drawn chart patterns, bullish and bearish version of each. Universal exit rule repeated throughout: **"always look left"** — take profit is the nearest prior peak/trough/rejection zone to the left.

- Indicators + exact settings:
  - **Fibonacci retracement** used with flags/pennants: expected retrace levels **0.382, 0.5, 0.618** — "when it's a high momentum it should retrace at a 382, sometimes it'll go down to the 0.5 or 618, which is the gold zone" [1:51–2:01]. Draw the fib across the impulse leg.
  - **RSI** for double tops/bottoms — length and levels **not stated**; only divergence is used [4:57–5:21].
- Per-pattern rules:
  - **Bull flag** [0:57–1:45]: big bullish impulse, then a small zig-zag bearish drift inside a channel. Entry = break of the upper channel. **Stop loss = halfway through the channel.** TP = look left / prior high.
  - **Bear flag** [2:06–2:35]: mirror. Entry on break of the lower channel, SL halfway through the channel, TP look left.
  - **Cup and handle** [2:36–3:16]: rounded bottom, rejection at a level (usually a fib level), then a dip of **about 50% of that range**, second rounded bottom → entry; SL and TP chart-pointed ("this is your stop loss", TP = "wherever the price did that"). He says he rarely uses it.
  - **Tight consolidation range breakout** [3:17–4:01]: price enters a range and consolidates very tightly, no dips in and out of the range. Entry on the range break. **SL above (bear: below) the range.** TP = look left, previous lows. **Minimum RR filter: "if you can't get more than a 2 to one on this, do not take this... you're looking more like 5 to one."**
  - **Bull pennant** [4:18–4:44]: like a bull flag but making higher lows, so the pullback forms a wedge rather than a parallel channel. "You want to wait until the end of this wedge"; entry on the break, SL just under the wedge, TP look left. Bear pennant is the mirror.
  - **Double top / double bottom** [4:51–5:21]: two touches of **exactly the same price level** plus an **RSI divergence** — double top = RSI makes a lower high; double bottom = RSI makes a massive low then a higher low.
  - **Head and shoulders** [5:22–6:16]: HTF only (1h/4h/daily). The two shoulders are "very close to one another" but need not be level ("it's not always proper posture"). **Entry = break of the neckline level.** TP = look left to the prior rejection zone; "usually these are big moves". Inverse H&S is the mirror.
  - **Falling wedge / rising wedge** [6:19–6:45]: entry on the break out of the wedge, **SL just below (above) the wedge**, TP look left.
  - **Ascending / descending triangles** [7:01–7:35]: called "my favourite price action strategy"; flat top with rising lows = buyers wearing sellers out (and mirror). No entry/stop/target given here — deferred to a separate linked video.
  - **Symmetrical triangle** [7:36–8:25]: hardest to read. Directional bias comes from **which direction price entered the triangle**: entered from big highs coming down → look for downside; entered from below → look for upside. He avoids it "until it's dramatically broken out in either direction", then scalps it.
- Filters he adds: the 2:1 minimum on the tight-range breakout; avoid symmetrical triangles until a decisive break; H&S is HTF-only.
