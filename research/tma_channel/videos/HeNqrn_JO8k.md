# When to Enter a Trade - The Right Way

## Mechanical rules (only what the video actually states)
- Indicators + exact settings: No moving averages, no oscillators. Tools used: trend lines (he calls them "my imaginary trend line", [1:01]), and the **Gann Box tool** used as a **PD array (premium/discount array)** — the only level that matters is the **50% level** ("this is above the 50% level and below the 50% level", [1:57]). Optional: **Heikin Ashi candles** on the 15m for entry timing.
- Setup/context required: A confirmed trend change / continuation via market structure. Downtrend is broken by a trend-line break "that is confirmed by a failure to make a lower low" ([1:03]). Then wait for the move that "broke previous structure" i.e. the swing that takes out the previous higher high and creates a new higher high ([1:25]–[1:34]). Bullish bias only after that break.
- Entry trigger: Draw the Gann-box/PD array over the **top and bottom of the swing that broke structure**; wait for price to pull back into the **discount half (below the 50% level)** and buy there. "using the top and the bottom of the move that broke structure you wait for a pullback into the lower 50% area and you get in on a trade" ([2:04]). Optional precision entry: switch the 15-minute chart to Heikin Ashi and enter on "the first hik and ashy candle that you get with **no wick on the bottom** confirming your bullish bias" ([4:22]).
- Stop loss: "your stop loss is below the PD array" ([2:13]) — i.e. below the low of the structure-breaking swing (the 0% of the box).
- Take profit: Trail. Baseline is a **1:2** trade; "imagine you were just going for a 1 to two once you hit a 1: one you can move your stop loss to break even" ([3:32]). After break-even, trail the stop / open take-profit to **the bottom of each successive PD array** as new structure-breaking swings form, until price stops you out ([3:39]–[3:53]).
- Filters he adds: **No-trade-day rule** — if the move that broke structure never pulls back into the discount (below-50%) half of its own PD array, that is a "no trading day". "the next move that broke structure price did not get into an area of discount so this is a no trading day ... no pullback into our area of discount no Trading day" ([2:18]–[2:31]). He also refuses to trade consolidation: "I don't like to trade periods of consolidation because I find them seriously annoying" ([0:47]). Getting stopped out is itself a regime signal: "getting stopped out on a trade means that your bull run is over and you should be looking for consolidation or a bare run" ([3:03]); then define the consolidation range as the high and low of the last swing and stand aside until price leaves it ([3:08]–[3:18]).
