# How To Trade a Divergence Properly
- id: udwkldark34 | views: 74000 | length: 324s

## Mechanical rules (only what the video actually states)
- Indicators + exact settings:
  - **RSI — "type in RSI, select the standard one from TradingView, not by anybody, just relative strength index"** [1:21]-[1:26]. **Levels 70 / 30**: "The standard levels of the RSI are 70 on the overbought end and 30 on the oversold end" [2:27]-[2:33]. Length never spoken.
  - **Parallel channel tool** — "if you click on your tools and click on Parallel channels... draw it on that trend line" [0:51]-[0:59]. Used for both trend definition and stop placement.
- Setup/context required:
  - Trend/channel context drawn first with trend lines and parallel channels [0:36]-[1:11].
  - A **regular divergence** at a reversal point: price makes a higher high while RSI makes a lower high (short), or price makes a lower low while RSI makes a higher low (long) [1:44]-[1:53], [4:16]-[4:23].
  - **Rejects a mere structure break as the signal:** "price made a high and then failed to make a higher high, that's where the market structure was broken — that's not what we're looking for. What we are looking for is price making a higher high and at that point rejecting" [1:36]-[1:48].
- Entry trigger — **the "secret sauce", stated three ways:**
  1. "I want to see not only a Divergence but I want to see **the first Divergence out of that range**" [2:40]-[2:46].
  2. "**I want to wait until we've gotten something in between the 70 30 range**" [2:46]-[2:50] — i.e. RSI must first exit 70/30, then return inside it.
  3. Entry is taken at that return: "once price loses that momentum and gets back into the normal range... that's where I want to get in on my trades" [3:00]-[3:12].
  - In the long example the entry candle is also specified: "on that rejection candle, huge Wick rejection, where inside of that 70 30 range — good to go, long position" [4:23]-[4:31].
  - Rationale given: "once we get out of the 70 30 range, meaning that the price is overbought, it will continue to be overbought because it's a momentum indicator" [2:54]-[3:00].
- Stop loss: **at the parallel channel**, sized ≈ **50 points on NAS/indices** — "stop loss at your channel, 50 points. 50 points on indices is a pretty decent stop loss, it'll keep you out of trouble when it comes to spreads, any type of slippage" [4:31]-[4:38]. Confirmed by the short example: "a one to four with a **50-point stop loss** on NAS" [3:43]-[3:46].
- Take profit: **scale out and trail to successive structure**, not a fixed target.
  - "originally set like a **one to two** because that's super safe and then you're gonna trail up to the previous structure up at the top" [4:42]-[4:49].
  - Trail mechanics: "if it hits there take a little bit off the table, move your stop loss to break even, move your next profit target to there... every single time you're taking a little bit off the table and moving your stop loss more and more into profit" [3:15]-[3:31].
  - Trail anchor: "all along the way you are trailing your profit with the current trend line" [4:55]-[4:57]; targets are the previous swing lows (short) / previous structure highs (long).
  - Results narrated: 1:4 on the short [3:43], 1:3 on the long [5:07].
- Filters he adds:
  - **Wick handling for divergence measurement:** "instead of measuring the extreme of the wick, [use] like half of it, because there's less volume in these wicks, there's more volume in the body" [3:56]-[4:05]; in the example he took the level "where the parallel channel was" [4:05].
  - Divergences must form **within/with respect to the 70/30 range** — "you have to wait for the divergences to form within that 70 30 range" [5:16]-[5:20].
  - Divergence + trend lines together are the stated method: "divergences and trend lines are like my favorite way to trade" [5:09]-[5:14].
