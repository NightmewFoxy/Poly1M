# Best Candlestick Patterns (GET BETTER ENTRIES)
- id: 2SheMv5EodY | views: 300000 | length: 568s
- market(s) shown: AUDUSD (Australian dollar / US dollar) [3:20]-[3:22]
- timeframe(s) taught: **1 hour** — "keep in mind though that this is a 1 hour time frame so you are going to be holding trades for a long time, this is not scalping" [5:33]-[5:41]

## Mechanical rules (only what the video actually states)
- Indicators + exact settings: **none — "always start with a naked chart"** [0:59]-[1:03]. Only horizontal S/R lines, drawn "across every single area that recently had some rejections and support, basically anywhere that there's a point" [1:16]-[1:29].
- Setup/context required:
  - Mark S/R levels first; the levels double as the take-profit grid — "use them as ladders... as the price starts going in between these lines these are going to be your takeprofit areas" [1:33]-[1:50].
  - **Every pattern only counts AT a key S/R level**, never mid-range: "you always have to wait for it to hit zones before you can get into a trade comfortably" [4:41]-[4:46]; "it's way better to set your trades at key support and resistance levels" [5:10]-[5:16].
  - Baseline assumption: "we should always be under the assumption that when price reaches a level that it's previously rejected at, that it's most likely going to reject at that point" [2:27]-[2:35].
- Entry trigger: **Only two of the five patterns are entry candles.**
  1. **Long-wick candle at a marked level → enter at the CLOSE of that candle.** "this my friends is a long wick candle hitting that level showing a massive rejection off of that zone; that right there is your entry at the close of that candle for a short position, targeting the next zone" [3:04]-[3:18].
  2. **Engulfing candle (he also calls it a momentum candle) at a key level.** Defined mechanically: it "literally engulfs the previous candle" [4:11]-[4:16]. Valid only at a level — "when a candle engulfs another candle at a previous support and resistance level it's way more important and significant than when it happens in the middle of a zone" [4:23]-[4:32]. He also takes the **retest after a level is broken**: "because we've already been in a trade that went through that level, coming back up to retest, this is going to be another good entry point" [5:16]-[5:23].
  3. **Inside bar — explicitly NOT an entry.** "this is not an entry candle, this is just a candle to signify a loss of momentum at that zone" [6:11]-[6:20].
  4/5. **Shrinking candles into a level** + **multiple long wicks at one level** — both are warning/confluence signals, not triggers [6:24]-[8:15].
- Stop loss: **below the engulfing candle** for the engulfing setup — stated by counter-example at [5:04]-[5:10] ("if you had set your stop loss below this engulfing candle right here you would have been stopped out" — the point being the level, not the candle, must be right). Example numbers given: **20 pip stop loss with a 92 pip take profit** [5:27]-[5:33]. No stop rule at all is given for the long-wick entry.
- Take profit: **the next marked S/R zone.** Long-wick example: **20 pips** to the nearest zone [3:17]-[3:22]; extendable to the **next zone down** where resistance was previously found, "taking this from a 20 pip trade to a 50 pip trade" [3:37]-[3:44]. Engulfing example: **92 pips** [5:27]-[5:33].
  - Priority stated: "your main takeprofit area should be that key level that you marked up" [3:44]-[3:48].
  - Trade-off stated: "those smaller zone trades are usually going to hit takeprofit faster, and if you want a long-term hold that you just need to monitor the trade and have a trailing stoploss on it and/or look for another long wick candle" [3:48]-[4:01].
  - "always look left when you're looking at your targets" [3:29]-[3:31].
- Filters he adds:
  - **Long wick is defined RELATIVE to neighbours, not absolutely:** "look at the surrounding candles and see what the average candle looks like in comparison to a long wick" [2:01]-[2:09]. This is the closest thing to a formula (wick vs local average) but no ratio is given.
  - **Set price alerts** at the marked levels rather than watching: "I highly advocate setting alerts, just drawing up at that level an alert and your phone sends you a message when it hits that level" [2:55]-[3:04].
  - **Confluence stacking is the thesis:** "if you can combine all five of these patterns in one level the assurance that you're going to have on that trade is far greater... the more confluences you have the more confidence you have in your trade" [8:43]-[9:03].
  - Patterns 4 and 5 are explicitly paired: shrinking candles into a zone usually coincide with multiple long wicks there [8:15]-[8:21]; both together are "almost guaranteed to show momentum shift in the opposite direction" [8:09]-[8:15].
