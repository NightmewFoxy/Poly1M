# Moving Average Crossover **IT DOES NOT WORK**
- id: bKl4FIMxQPE | views: 232000 | length: 640s
- market(s) shown: BTCUSD ("does this work on crypto? Yes, we're going to do it on bitcoin US dollar")
- timeframe(s) taught: 1h ([2:23] "this is Bitcoin US dollar on the one hour chart"); S/R also built on the 1h

## Mechanical rules (only what the video actually states)
- Indicators + exact settings:
  - **21 and 200 SMOOTHED moving averages** ([1:59] "21 smooth moving average and the 200 smooth moving average up on this chart, signified in white and red"), plus references to a **21/50** crossover at [2:26]. He states his preference: [2:07] "I like smooth moving averages because I find that the price respects those the most."
  - **Daryl Guppy GMMA** (Guppy Multiple Moving Average): **6 short-term EMAs** representing retail traders, plus a set of higher-timeframe EMAs (drawn in red) representing institutions. Exact periods are never stated. Its reversal signal = the short EMA group crossing over the long EMA group ([4:04]–[4:44]).
  - The channel's free **TMA RSI divergence indicator**: solid yellow RSI line, a **50 level** line across the middle, plus a moving average of the RSI that "would basically show you the RSI 50 level across multiple time frames" ([8:20]–[8:39]). RSI length unstated. Plots regular and hidden bullish/bearish divergences.
- Setup/context required: The video's thesis is a **negative rule** — do not use MA crossovers as an entry. [2:57] "this is why the moving average crossover strategy is literally one of the worst ways to trade. If you do get in profit, it's going to be very, very small and then usually consolidate until you eventually closed it in a negative amount." Reason given: [1:25]–[1:37] "in order for the 200 and the 50 moving average to eventually cross over, that means that the price had already moved a ton before that cross even happened... by the time that you got in on your trade, the move was already over and starting to retrace." He grants the GMMA crossover gives an **earlier** signal than the 21/200 cross but says it is still late ([4:44]–[5:11], [9:24]–[9:31]).
- Entry trigger (his replacement): a **confluence stack**, entered on a **big-ass (engulfing/momentum) candle** — [7:12] "you guys know I love trading big ass candles. They are the big momentum shift moves that we're looking for as our entries because it gives you the tightest stop-loss." The stack he names:
  1. **A break AND retest of a hand-drawn trendline** — the retest rejection candle is the entry. [5:59] "when you see something like that off of a break and retest... you could have gotten in right here as this candle crossed its previous level of support because you see this long rejection." He contrasts a break with no retest (continued down) against a break with a retest that produced "this huge huge rejection".
  2. **A rejection wick off a horizontal 1h support/resistance level**, ideally a **double bottom / double top** ([7:26]–[8:20]).
  3. **An RSI divergence** on the TMA indicator — his shown example is two lows where the second RSI low is higher while price made a double bottom ([8:51]–[9:06]).
  4. **Three consecutive big momentum candles** breaking out of the prior downtrend as confirmation ([7:34]–[7:58]).
  [9:48] "for me I would have gotten in right there [at the] break of a trendline. I'm not worried about a moving average crossover."
- Stop loss: not stated numerically. Only the qualitative claim that big-ass-candle entries give "the tightest stop-loss" ([7:18]) and that crossover entries get stopped out because the stop "was too tight" ([2:47]).
- Take profit: **the next horizontal support/resistance level.** [6:10] "once that happens, what zone do you target? You target your next level of support and resistance." He also concedes the MA crossover has one legitimate use: as a **hold-longer confirmation**, not an entry — [9:52] "this is nice to let me know that I can actually hold my trade [to] this next support and resistance zone"; [10:01] "moving average crossovers have their place but you need to know the good stuff first before you can use this, to hold your trade longer."
- Filters he adds: none numeric — no session, day-of-week or news filter in this video. Direction bias comes from the RSI 50 line ([8:39] "this is going to be your indication of where the trend is going").
