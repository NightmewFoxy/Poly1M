## Mechanical rules (only what the video actually states)
- Indicators + exact settings: none for entry. Trailing-stop moving averages named: 50 MA first ([7:07] "a 50 moving average"), then corrected at [10:12]–[10:22] to prefer the 100 MA ("use a 50 or a 100 in this case the 100 worked a lot better so maybe you should just do that and ignore my 50 statement earlier"). MA type (SMA/EMA/SMMA) unstated in this video.
- Setup/context required: A completed Wyckoff accumulation range after a big move down. Sequence to be labelled left-to-right: Phase A = preliminary support (PS, can be more than one) → selling climax (SC, at the absolute low, "usually comes accompanied with long wicks" [8:12]) → automatic rally (AR, identified as the break of the descending trendline of lower highs [8:26]–[8:29]) → secondary test (ST). Phase B = an additional secondary test; redraw a narrower support line at the bottom of the ST wick and a resistance line at the top of the push after the ST ([4:20]–[4:34]). Phase C = the last point of support (LPS), which may wick below the phase-B support line. Phase D = higher lows / rising LPS's and the breakout of the range.
- Entry trigger: In phase D, the simultaneous SOS (sign of strength — break out of the range) and BU/LPS. [6:23] "so basically you break out of that range and you're putting your long position right here". Long only (this video covers accumulation; distribution/shorts deferred to a later video).
- Stop loss: [6:27] "your stop loss below that previous high" — stated exactly that way in the transcript (ambiguous/likely misspoken; see vague section).
- Take profit: Normally he advocates 1:2 R, but for a Wyckoff range breakout he says 1:4 or 1:5 are "completely suitable" ([6:52]–[6:58]). Rule given: at 1:3, "take most of your profit off the table and use a trailing stop loss" on a 50 MA ([7:01]–[7:07]), amended to the 100 MA at [10:12]. He claims his own example ran 1:5, and holding the 100-MA trail would have been ~1:9 ([10:28]).
- Filters he adds: none numeric. Qualitative consolidation identification: "you're looking at seesaw up and down you're looking at high volumes" ([11:28]). No session, day-of-week or news filter.
