## Mechanical rules (only what the video actually states)
This is his "everything" video and he explicitly says he does **not** trade one fixed strategy: "i don't trade one specific strategy i know pretty much everything about day trading" ([0:16]). What follows is his stated workflow order.

- **Step 1 — Naked-chart S/R, before any indicator.** On the 1h, mark levels "where the price rejects or bounces off of" with the horizontal line tool ([1:55]). Snap them to **round/even numbers** — "these prices right here are usually even numbers if you can adjust it just a little bit to be those even numbers these are what is known as psychological price levels" ([2:08]). Then **check the same levels on the daily** to confirm they have held historically ([2:40]–[3:14]). "this is before i put any indicators on here" ([3:17]).
- **Step 2 — Market structure / trend.** Downtrend = lower low, lower high, lower low, lower high. Structure is **broken** when instead of a lower high it makes a **higher high then a higher low then a higher high** ([3:55]–[4:10]). Reversal warning = "the price made a higher low but failed to make that higher high breaking that market structure" ([4:15]).
- **Step 3 — Fibonacci on the retracement.** Draw the fib "from top to bottom of that move ... you're looking for one clean long movement" ([5:05]). The **"gold zone" is 0.5 to 0.618** — "this is the most ideal zone this is known as the gold zone between the 0.5 and the 0.618" ([5:17]). When price retraces into it, "it's almost assured to go down to the same level that it was" ([5:31]). **Targets: the negative 0.382 and the negative 0.618 fib extensions** ([5:36]–[5:50]). TP1 = the prior swing extreme, because of double-bottom risk ([5:52]–[6:04]).
- **Impulse-severity rule for which fib level to expect:** "a very sharp movement like that is likely to retrace at a lower fibonacci level like a 382 as opposed to a 0.5 or a 0.618 ... because that move was less aggressive" ([7:57]–[8:15]). Restated at [6:56]: "all of these options are dependent on the strength of the trend".
- **Step 4 — Pivot confirmation via candle formations.** "how do you know where these pivot points are ... it is all candle formations" ([7:23]). Named patterns: rounded top ending in a big engulfing candle ([7:52]), **wedge breakout continuation** ([9:00]), and the **tweezer top**, defined precisely: "the closing of one candle was the exact opening of the next candle and they have the exact same candle wick length" ([9:20]–[9:30]).
- **Step 5 — ONLY NOW add indicators, purely as confluence.** "once you've done all of this and you've decided that this is your entry but you want a little bit more confidence ... at this point and only at this point should you start using indicators" ([11:03]–[11:18]). The four confluences he accepts: "either an engulfing candle or it rejecting off of a moving average seeing an overbought territory in a stochastic rsi or a divergence on a traditional rsi" ([11:20]).
- Indicators + exact settings: **his own "TMA overlay"** — "we have the 200 moving average the 50 moving average and the 21 moving average **these are all smoothed moving averages** they're the ones that i prefer i've changed the settings a bit just to do this just to show me **three line strikes** and the 21 the 50 and the 200 everything else is unticked" ([11:36]–[11:55]). Plus **Stochastic RSI** (settings unstated) and **traditional RSI** (length unstated; he reads absolute values 20, 29, 33, 37 off it).
- **Three line strike, defined:** "three consecutive candles in one direction and then one engulfing candle in the opposite direction" ([12:02]).
- Entry trigger (the fully worked short at [12:47]–[13:10]): price retraces to the **0.382**, prints a **three line strike** whose engulfing candle rejects **exactly off the 21 SMMA**, with a **Stochastic RSI crossover down out of overbought** — "that is your entry you're taking your short position". Four stacked confluences: fib level (1), three-line-strike engulfing at that level (2), the 21 SMMA at that level (3), stoch RSI overbought crossover (4) ([12:13]–[12:46]).
- Stop loss: "**your stop loss is going to be the next moving average up maximum**" ([13:12]). Conditional widening: "if this were a less violent move down i would move my stop loss up here because i would be expecting a gold zone retracement but because it's retracing at the 382 the next moving average up is completely fine". Alternative: "you can even do the **50 level on the fibonacci tool**" — on 15m GBPUSD that was a **17 pip stop loss** "and that is perfectly acceptable" ([13:28]–[13:38]).
- Take profit: **Check R BEFORE entering** — "before you actually enter your trade you need to see if your take profit area is going to be good enough to actually take the trade if you're only looking to get 11 pips out of it you're not getting a good risk to reward ratio" ([13:41]). Worked numbers: targeting the previous low = **1:2.19** ("this is plenty especially if that's being your take profit one area"); running to the **-0.382** extension, which was also a known S/R level, = **1:4.3** ([13:54]–[14:26]). Prefer whichever S/R level is nearest the fib target: "these support and resistance levels they are the easiest price targets to hit" ([9:41]).
- Position management: "you can actually **close 90% of your trade** here ... and you can train yourself to take the other 10% of that remaining trade that's still active and trailing stop loss it" ([14:28]–[14:46]).
- Swing trailing method (attributed to swing traders, and explicitly not his own style): move the stop to each successive **retracement level / lower high** as the trend progresses; the trail-out becomes the take profit. His example: **419 pips over 15 days**, versus "a normal person scalping for 20 or 30 pips" ([10:09]–[11:01]).
- **His personal default trade** (stated separately from the above): "i like to primarily go off of the **200 period moving average** i like to get that **first move off of it** in one direction whether i'm getting in early or a little bit after it crosses over **once i see a nice movement past the 200 i'm out** i'm done with the trade because i'm assuming the price is too far away from the 200 and is going to go back to it" ([14:48]–[15:13]).
- Divergences (both types defined): **Regular** — price at the same level while "it created a low here at 29 and then created a higher low at 33 that's your divergence to give you an indication that the price might break its previous market structure and reverse" ([15:34]–[15:51]). **Hidden** — "hidden divergences are trend continuation divergences ... this is a lower high but you can see on the rsi ... this one's going down this one's going up this is a hidden bearish divergence rejecting off of the 200" ([15:53]–[16:26]). Double-bottom + RSI divergence pairing is required for the TP1/reversal read at [6:04]–[6:28].
- Filters he adds: **Trade one pair only until mastered** — "this is why i suggest only focusing on one pair and until you've mastered that do not go on to another one" ([3:25]). Do analysis on **naked charts** and clear indicators off afterwards ([16:52]–[17:03]). Fundamentals/news override algos: "algorithmic trading models do not take into account the fomo mentality ... or the fud mentality" ([17:56]–[18:30]).
