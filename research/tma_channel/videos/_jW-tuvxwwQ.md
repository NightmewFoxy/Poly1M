## Mechanical rules (only what the video actually states)
- Indicators + exact settings: **"Happy Trails"** (his community's proprietary indicator) — prints a right-side-up smiley face for a buy entry and an upside-down smiley face for a sell entry. **No settings given.** He notes four sibling indicators with the same role: Brute Force (rocket ships), Stair Master (sneakers), Outback (kangaroos) — "every single one of these strategies has specific rules that you need to follow" [3:14–3:29].
- Setup/context required:
  - Zoom in and find **peaks/troughs where price rejected with a long wick**; draw a **rectangle box** over each — "when you get a long wick rejection like this, what you can do is draw a rectangle box; this is a zone of support and resistance, supply and demand" [0:48–0:59]. Same on the bottom of the range: "on the bottom of the range we have these long wicks again, drawing up another zone" [1:02–1:09].
  - **Price must have travelled into one of those boxes and reacted off it.** "Just wait and be patient for price to hit these specific levels and react off of these specific levels. Do not jump the gun" [1:33–1:38].
- Entry trigger: the Happy Trails smiley signal **printed while price is inside a marked zone** — long when the signal is at the lower part of the range, short when the signal prints as price rejects the upper zone [1:41–1:55, 2:13–2:29].
- Stop loss: **below the range/zone** for longs [1:55]; **above the range/zone** for shorts [2:29].
- Take profit: **1 : 2 risk-to-reward is the stated target** — "enter in a long position, stop loss below the range, with a one to two risk to reward ratio, or you can target this zone again... just focus on one to twos; one to twos consistently will get you to be a profitable day trader" [1:55–2:08]. **Fallback: if a 1:2 doesn't fit inside the range, accept 1 : 1.5** — "you want to target a one to two that is below your range, so what you need to do is be okay with a one to one point five; you need to have a specific take profit area that works in your confined area" [2:31–2:43].
- Filters he adds:
  - **Do not take breakout entries** — the explicit counter-example: "if you would have been impatient and you're a breakout trader you would have gotten into a long position here and you would have gotten smoked" [2:20–2:27].
  - Ignore mid-range signals: "stop looking at all of this noise in the middle and just focus on areas where price has previously rejected and go off of signals in those areas" [3:02–3:12].
  - Prefer breaks-and-retests, structure, double bottoms/double tops [2:43–2:51].
