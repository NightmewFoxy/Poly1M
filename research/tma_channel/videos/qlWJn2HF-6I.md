## Mechanical rules (only what the video actually states)
- Indicators + exact settings: **none.** Pure price action; the only chart object is a hand-drawn **rectangular box** across the base zone [5:34]-[5:41].
- Setup/context required: Identify a **supply/demand zone** with this specific signature:
  1. A **small base** — a short consolidation of one to a few candles (explicitly NOT a long consolidation: "which is why the small bases are important, not the long periods of consolidation... but short bases" [7:23]-[7:33]).
  2. Followed by a **very sharp move away** from that base. The one numeric definition of "sharp" in the video: **steeper than 45 degrees** — "this is a very sharp move, this is more than a 45 degree angle downwards" [5:27]-[5:32].
  3. He names two zone sub-types [5:07]-[5:20]: "wide platform" bases and "narrow little flimsy" ones, likened to diving boards. Also distinguishes a **single-candle** sharp move [5:25]-[5:27] from a **multi-candle small base** [9:06]-[9:13].
  4. Draw a **long rectangular box across that base zone** and extend it right, then wait for price to return [5:32]-[5:47].
- Entry trigger: **Price returns to the zone and prints a sharp one- or two-candle rejection in the opposite direction.** "as the price comes back up to that zone and we get this sharp one or two candle rejection in the opposite direction, that right there is your short position" [5:49]-[6:01].
- Stop loss: **Just above the peak of the zone — 2 to 3 pips beyond it.** "your stop loss is going to be above the peak, just barely, just above the peak of that, maybe two or three pips" [6:01]-[6:08]. (Mirror for longs implied, not stated.)
- Take profit: **Look left; place TP where price has crossed the most.** "your target is simply going to be look left and where the most price has crossed, right there" [6:08]-[6:12]. Expected size: **35-40 pips** on 30m [6:12]-[6:17].
  - Management: **close 100% at TP by default.** "if you want to leave a runner close 90 of your position, but I usually advocate that you just close 100 of your position at your take profit and you look for your next trade" [6:24]-[6:34].
- Filters he adds:
  - **Multi-candle small-base zones > single-candle zones.** "those trades that have the base, the small base where there are multiple positions that the banks got in at, that is a much stronger move than the quick single candle one... more trapped traders means a longer bigger move off of it" [9:06]-[9:26]. This is a rankable, testable preference.
  - **Long periods of consolidation are disqualified** as bases [7:23]-[7:33].
  - **Emergency exit rule:** "if the price is moving against you very very sharply, please get the [expletive] out of your position... admit your mistake, get out of your position, prevent yourself from having a massive loss" [8:51]-[9:04].
  - The **V-shape** is the tell of institutional entry: "this v-shape is what you want to look for, these v-shapes only happen when banks get into the market" [2:10]-[2:16].
  - Zones can be re-traded on repeat visits: "when price reaches that again and we get these massive candles off of it you can trade this zone again" [6:41]-[6:50].
  - Long-side variant described at [7:52]-[8:23]: banks accumulate in two small tranches, spike price up to trap/liquidate, bring price back **into the zone one last time**, then rally.
