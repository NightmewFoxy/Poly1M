## Mechanical rules (only what the video actually states)
- Indicators + exact settings: **none** — pure SMC price action, no indicator used anywhere in the video.
- Zone definition (three ways to draw the same supply/demand zone) [2:06–3:03]:
  - **Range supply/demand** — mark the entire range of the last impulse before the reversal (the example is 3 candles). Entry at the near edge of the zone. Lower RR "because your stop loss needs to be above the supply zone".
  - **Pivot supply/demand** — only **the last bullish candle before the bearish candle that made the pivot** (mirror for demand). Higher RR. "Most smart money concepts traders choose pivot supply and demand zones" [4:06].
  - **Fractal supply/demand** — only **the top wick of the bearish movement**. Extremely high RR "but the chance of you being tagged into a trade is much lower, so you will miss some trades" [2:55–3:03].
- Three demand-zone shapes to hunt on the HTF [4:10–6:13]:
  - **STB wick** (sell-to-buy wick): the HTF candle whose high was rejected and whose successor pushed down then rejected up — zone = "from the top of this wick to the bottom of this wick". Bearish mirror = **BTS wick** (buy-to-sell).
  - **Inside bar**: an HTF inside bar = a short ranging period on the LTF before continuation.
  - **Large wick**: on the LTF this resolves into come-down → reject → retest → continue.
  - All three are to be identified on higher time frames, then refined on the lower time frame for entry.
- Setup/context required (institutional zones) [6:14–8:33]:
  - **Only mark zones that led to a break of structure** — "you only want to focus on zones that lead to breaks of structure" [8:20]. Zones that didn't break structure are ignored.
  - A HTF swing that rejects and then breaks a previous high = **confirmed demand zone**; a swing that breaks previous structure downward = **valid supply zone**.
  - **Flip zone:** in a clear downtrend, when price rallies through the HTF structure and through the supply zones, that broken supply becomes a valid demand zone; wait for the retrace into it and follow the new trend [7:53–8:16].
- Entry trigger: wait for price to **return into the marked HTF zone**, then drop to the lowest time frame and take the **change of character** that prints near the zone — "the lowest time frame is where you're going to find your change of characters... that's where you want to look at entering your trades" [7:40–7:52].
- Location filter (the one numeric rule in the video): **enter only in the discounted half of the leg — below the 50% level** for longs (premium/above 50% for shorts). "You don't want to enter the trade here or at the top, you want to wait and be patient and have price come back into that demand zone, which is also a discounted zone... the 50 level, this is the only half of this area that you want to get into" [7:01–7:17]; repeated at [9:00] "areas of discount below the 50 percent level".
- Stop loss: only stated implicitly for range zones — "your stop loss needs to be above the supply zone" [2:30].
- Take profit: **never stated.** No R multiple, no target rule anywhere in the video.
- Filters he adds: higher-time-frame zones outrank lower ones ("higher time frame levels of demand hold more significance"); "the more significant the structure, the more significant the zone" [6:34].
