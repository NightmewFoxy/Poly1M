# support and resistance / order blocks / supply demand - what is the difference
- id: x7_tm8co0X8 | views: 114000 | length: 343s
- market(s) shown: **US30** — "what we have here is US 30 on the one day chart, so each one of these candles is one day" [0:15]-[0:21]
- timeframe(s) taught: **daily**

## Mechanical rules (only what the video actually states)
This is a definitions/concept video. Its thesis is that the three named concepts are the same thing: "Order blocks, supply and demand, support and resistance generally are all the same thing... whether it's smart money concepts or price action or ICT, essentially it is all the same stuff" [5:03]-[5:21].

- Indicators + exact settings:
  - **None required.** Workflow tools only: switch chart type **from candles to lines** to find S/R ("This makes everything way easier — you can see the bottoms, the tops, you don't have to worry about wicks" [0:30]-[0:41]), then the trend-line tool for horizontal levels [0:41]-[0:48], then switch back to candlesticks to read rejections [2:00]-[2:06].
  - Optional: **an off-the-shelf order-block indicator** — "type in order blocks into the indicators tab and choose one that you like. Usually the ones that have the highest number right here are the ones that you should pick" [2:30]-[2:41] (i.e. sort by likes; he "just picked the first one"). Name and settings unstated.
- Setup/context required:
  - **Use zones, not lines:** "instead of just simply using a line, you can now start using zones" — the zone "gives you a little bit more wiggle room" [1:25]-[1:43].
  - **Order-block definition (the one crisp definition in the video):** "when price pulls back... as price pulled back and broke through it again, **the push of price to break through that previous high is now an area known as an order block**" [3:16]-[3:34]. He repeats it three times with examples at [3:34]-[3:53].
  - Resistance flips to support after a break and retest [0:54]-[1:02].
  - Trend context for order-block entries: bullish order blocks form on the way up while price keeps making higher highs and higher lows [2:53]-[3:16].
- Entry trigger: **retest of the order block / zone, with a strong rejection candle, in the direction of the trend.**
  - "instead of getting into a trade in this area, you can wait until it **leaves that area with a strong rejection** and have a bias of the direction of your trade" [1:43]-[1:54].
  - "You don't want to get into trades unless you see a nice strong rejection from that area" [2:18]-[2:25].
  - "Most of the time when price comes back down to an area that is an order block, it rejects and continues up" [3:53]-[4:01].
- Stop loss: **below the order block, or below the wick/swing** — "with a stop loss below the order block or below a little wick or swing" [4:09]-[4:13].
- Take profit: **1 : 2 risk-to-reward** — "target a one to two, you'd be pretty safe and win a majority of your trades if you're holding on to a trade with a trend" [4:13]-[4:21]. Restated for supply/demand: "you can have your entries up here — this will get you a one to two risk to reward ratio" [4:30]-[4:36].
- Filters he adds:
  - **Three supply/demand zone-drawing conventions, with their R consequence** [4:36]-[4:55]:
    1. **Range supply/demand** — the whole zone (lowest R);
    2. **Pivot** — "literally just uses the last candle... which will get you a higher risk to reward ratio";
    3. **Fractal** — "just uses the wick"; "Fractal obviously gets you the highest risk to reward".
  - Trade with the trend when holding order-block entries [4:16]-[4:21].
  - Caveat on level reliability: "support and resistance levels, as you can see, don't always get respected — they break through, they come up, and they get retested later. But over time you see that these areas continue to get respected over and over again" [1:10]-[1:25].

## Vague / untestable / chart-pointed claims
- [0:41]-[0:54] "if you simply take your trend line tool and draw out these areas, you can see that this was an area where the price did not want to break through" — level selection is entirely by eye on a line chart; no touch count, no lookback, no tolerance.
- [1:25]-[1:43] "start using zones... gives you a little bit more wiggle room" — **no zone width rule** (no ATR multiple, no % of price, no wick-to-body convention). The zone width directly sets both the entry and the stop, so this is a load-bearing gap.
- [1:43]-[2:25] "**a nice strong rejection**" — the entry trigger, repeated three times, and never quantified (no body/wick ratio, no candle-size threshold, no close-location rule).
- [2:30]-[2:41] "choose one that you like... usually the ones that have the highest number are the ones that you should pick" — order-block detection is outsourced to an arbitrary third-party script chosen by popularity. Different scripts mark different zones, so this is not reproducible.
- [3:53]-[4:01] "**most of the time** when price comes back down to an order block, it rejects and continues up" — no frequency given, no sample, no market.
- [4:13]-[4:21] "you'd be pretty safe and win a majority of your trades" — unquantified win-rate claim.
- [4:36]-[4:55] The three zone types are named but only the pivot ("the last candle") and fractal ("just uses the wick") are described; "range supply like this" is chart-pointed with no definition of the range's boundaries.
- No timeframe rule beyond the daily chart shown, no session filter, no news filter, no position sizing, no invalidation/time-stop.
- [5:24]-[5:29] Defers supply/demand depth to a prior video ("I just posted it prior to this one") — that video, not this one, presumably carries the zone-drawing detail.
