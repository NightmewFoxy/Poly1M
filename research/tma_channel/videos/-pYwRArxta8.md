# Trend Reversals Earn Huge Profits

- timeframe(s) taught: explicitly timeframe-agnostic — "works on the 1-minute, works on the 5-minute, works on the weekly" [6:17]-[6:22]. Main worked example is WEEKLY; second example is 15m.

## Mechanical rules (only what the video actually states)
- Indicators + exact settings: RSI only. Length and overbought/oversold levels NEVER stated, but actual RSI readings are quoted: 79 (then "80") at the first high and 72 at the higher high [1:03]-[1:26]. Divergence is read off RSI peaks, not off a fixed level.
- Setup/context required (all of these, in order):
  1. Price trending in one direction; draw a trend line. **A trend line is only VALID once it has THREE touches** [0:44]-[0:53]. This is the one hard numeric rule in the video.
  2. Regular bearish divergence on RSI across the highs: price makes a higher high, RSI makes a lower high. He stresses divergences can persist/re-form across successive highs — a new higher high may cancel one divergence while a wider high-to-high divergence remains valid [1:57]-[2:24].
  3. Trend line must be BROKEN. "price will not break this uptrend until that trend line is broken and retested, or just broken with a lot of momentum" [1:35]-[1:45].
  4. Market structure break: bullish structure = low, high, higher low, higher high, higher low, higher high; "if it fails to create that higher high, that means that market structure is broken" [3:18]-[3:35]. Need BOTH the failed higher high AND the broken uptrend line before looking for shorts.
  5. Preferred (higher-probability) version: also wait for price to break below the prior swing low / "liquidity zone" where the long-entry stop losses sit, because those stops are sell orders that cascade price [2:39]-[3:18], [4:26]-[4:36].
- Entry trigger: short on the RETEST of the broken trend line ("It's best to always wait for break and a retest" [1:45]-[1:47]; restated [4:36] and [6:04]-[6:08]). A second, explicitly LOWER-probability variant: enter preemptively after the rejection of the failed-higher-high area, before liquidity is taken [3:42]-[4:00].
- Stop loss: above the swing high (the failed higher high) [1:28]-[1:30], [3:48]-[3:50]. Then TRAILED: requires "a clear, definitive swing up and a swing down", then move stop to break even; each time price swings past its previous low, move the stop to that new swing point — "meaning that it is a 100% risk-free trade" [4:56]-[5:18].
- Take profit: target the previous swing low [1:32]-[1:35], [6:08]-[6:11]. Further targets: "liquidity below wicks to the left" (i.e. prior wick lows) [1:57] equivalent — stated at [4:41]-[4:54] as producing better R.
- Filters he adds:
  - Waiting for the liquidity sweep gives a smaller stop and better R: he quotes 1:7.6 (waited) versus 1:6 (early entry) on the weekly example [4:41]-[4:54].
  - Entering too early without momentum = "you can get stuck in a trade for a very long time" [4:20]-[4:29].
  - Quoted results: weekly example 4,055 pips over 1,358 days [5:42]-[5:51]; 15m example 18-pip stop, 194-pip take profit, R = 1:10 [6:11]-[6:17].
  - He states he personally prefers scalping over swing trading because of the holding time [5:23]-[5:29].
