## Mechanical rules (only what the video actually states)
This is a framework/confluence video answering a viewer question ("sigma"), not a single codified setup. It does contain his personal indicator stack.
- Indicators + exact settings (the most useful content here):
  - **RSI** — used for divergence and for range position. Length and levels NEVER stated.
  - **200 SMOOTHED moving average (SMMA 200)** — "I like to have a 200 smoothed moving average on my chart to keep my trend analysis in the right direction" [2:32]-[2:39]. This is the one exact indicator setting in the video (length 200, type = smoothed/SMMA).
  - **Happy Trail** — his proprietary paid indicator, described as optional here [2:42]-[2:44], but it supplies the entry candle in the 5m example.
  - He states indicators are step 4 and **optional** — "this is optional for those of you that prefer naked trading" [2:24]-[2:32].
  - His stated go-to strategy: **divergences** — "my go-to trading strategy is using divergences; I want to catch the full swing of the trend" [2:44]-[3:02].
- Setup/context required — his 4-step stacking process [1:24]-[3:02]:
  1. **Structure and trend (top-down)**: start on 1h or 4h; ask "is the market trending or ranging?"
  2. **Key levels**: mark previous highs and lows, break-and-retest zones, fair value gaps, liquidity pools.
  3. **Candlestick confirmation** = the entry trigger: rejection wicks, engulfing candles, breaks of structure, or the **three-line strike** (named as his favourite).
  4. **Indicators for confirmation** (optional): RSI, SMMA 200, Happy Trail.
  - Rule over the whole stack: "you need to look for agreement, not contradictions" [3:02]-[3:06].
- Entry trigger (worked 5m example, long): a Happy Trail signal after a **double-bottom rejection that broke the previous downtrend** [5:30]-[5:36]. Earlier 15m version: enter on the candle after the double-bottom wick-down retest + strong rejection + RSI divergence, "stop loss below" [4:22]-[4:38].
- Stop loss: **below the previous swing low** (5m example) [5:38]-[5:41]; "stop loss below" the double-bottom wick in the 15m example.
- Take profit: **look left for your target — the previous swing high** [5:36]-[5:38]. Resulting R in the example: **1:1.8**.
- Filters he adds — the R-multiple veto, which is the hardest rule in the video:
  - **"You always want a 1:1 or higher"** — he rejects a setup whose next S/R target gives only 0.62 R: "the next level of support and resistance is a 0.62 risk-to-reward, not worth my risk" [4:40]-[4:48].
  - **RSI position veto**: "seeing the RSI that we're at the top of the range, we don't want to get into that trade" [4:48]-[4:55].
  - **Don't enter after the move has already gone** [4:55]-[4:58].
  - **Cap the number of confluences: "don't get addicted to needing seven different confluences... pick three to four key confluences that you trust"** [5:44]-[5:59].
  - Session timing is listed as a confluence category [1:13]-[1:15] but no session, time or timezone is ever given. He notes the good example entry "would have been right at market open" giving nearly 1:2 R [4:58]-[5:08] — which market open is not said.
