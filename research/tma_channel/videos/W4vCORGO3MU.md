## Mechanical rules (only what the video actually states)
This is a live trade-along, so the rules are stated as *confluences* rather than as
a numbered system. He explicitly enumerates them at [3:52]-[4:48].

- Indicators + exact settings:
  - **Gann box tool**, used to build a **PD array** — anchored **from the previous
    low to the highest point of the swing that broke that structure**
    ([0:17]-[0:46]). No other Gann settings given.
  - **New York midnight line** — a vertical line at midnight US, plus a horizontal
    line at the price at which it opened ([0:52]-[1:04]).
  - **RSI** — length/levels never stated; used only for divergence and for
    overbought/oversold reads ([2:50]-[3:54]).
  - **Fibonacci retracement**, dropped mid-trade "to see if maybe it would hit a fib
    retracement and then continue down" ([5:53]-[6:00]) — no levels named.
- Setup/context required (the short): downtrend structure of **lower highs and
  lower lows** ([3:00]-[3:07]); previous structure already broken to the downside.
- Entry trigger — his four stated confluences for the short ([4:24]-[4:41]):
  1. **Price broke previous structure** (BOS).
  2. **Price pulled back into the upper half of the PD array** — "if I'm going for
     a sell I'm looking for a **premium price**" ([4:07]-[4:16]).
  3. **An indecision candle** — "not a dogee, not a hammer, just indecisive"
     ([1:10]-[1:24]); he entered on that candle.
  4. **A hidden bearish divergence**: price makes a **lower high** while **RSI makes
     a higher high** ([3:24]-[3:54]).
  - He notes a **fifth** confluence he skipped: "it would have been better for me to
    wait for **the engulfing candle** ... but I was simply being impatient"
    ([4:41]-[4:52]).
  - Extra context noted: price came up to **grab liquidity above the New York
    midnight open line** before cascading down ([1:02]-[1:10], [4:16]-[4:24]).
- Stop loss:
  - Initial risk on the short = **$2,000** ([2:37]-[2:42]).
  - **Break-even-ish trail rule:** "because I'm already a one to one in profit I can
    move my stop loss to just above that swing" — this cut risk from **$2,000 to
    $730** ([2:30]-[2:45]). So: **at +1R, move stop to just above the most recent
    swing high** (for a short).
  - Governing principle stated at [6:32]-[6:54]: "**your stop loss should negate
    exactly what is being said** — so if you think that your trade is going against
    you, it hits your stop loss, and once it does go against you, you were wrong,
    and so you should be planning on taking the **opposite position**."
- Take profit:
  - Target **1:2 R** ([2:00]-[2:02]). He set the TP to a round **$4,000** using
    TradeLocker's position tool ([2:04]-[2:27]).
  - Alternative/safer target he names: **the previous low point** ("the safe bet for
    my take profit should be actually right here at this low Point assuming that
    worst case scenario we fall into consolidation", [1:48]-[2:00]).
  - On the reversal long: **stop loss set "somewhere comfortable, not right below
    the wick"** and **take profit exactly at the top of the PD array range**
    ([7:04]-[7:24]).
- Filters he adds: none stated — no session window, no day filter, no news filter
  in this video.
- Trade management discipline: "when I'm in a trade and I don't want to touch it I
  go do a hobby or other things — check emails, work, mow the lawn"
  ([8:52]-[9:00]). The trade he did **not** touch was the winner.
